# ❤️ Affective Understanding

🧬 **Core meme:** Emotions are signals about needs, so name them precisely.

Understand emotions: what they are, what drives them, and how to recognise them in oneself and others. Emotions are the brain's fast appraisals of what matters: signals about needs, threats and goals. Understanding them turns "I feel bad" into information that can guide action.

## The model

- **Appraisal:** emotions come from how a situation is interpreted against needs and goals (loss → sadness, threat → fear, blocked goal or unfairness → anger, violated standard → guilt or shame).
- **Components:** body sensations, thoughts, urges to act, expression.
- **Granularity:** people with a precise emotional vocabulary regulate better.
- **Mixed and masked:** emotions blend (relief and sadness) and are hidden (anger over hurt, humour over anxiety).
- **Emotion shapes thinking:** fear narrows attention; good mood broadens it; strong emotion reduces careful reasoning.

## For people

- **Build vocabulary:** from "angry" to irritated, resentful, betrayed, indignant; from "sad" to disappointed, lonely, grieving. Name the specific one.
- **Trace the appraisal:** what happened, what did I make it mean, what need is at stake?
- **Read others** from several cues together (words, tone, face, posture, behaviour change, context) and treat the reading as a guess to check ("You seem frustrated; is that right?").
- **Look under the surface:** anger often protects hurt or fear; ask about the softer feeling.
- **Mind the effect on decisions:** avoid big decisions in the grip of a strong emotion; sleep on it.

For staying calm and responding well in the moment, use `emotional-intelligence` in interpersonal. For persistent low mood or anxiety, see `mindset-resilience` in self-improvement and suggest professional support where it fits.

## For Claude

- **Read the emotional tone** of a message from its words, punctuation, length and context, as a guess.
- **Respond to the feeling as well as the content:** acknowledge frustration before fixing; match gravity to grief; do not answer distress with a cheerful list.
- **Do not overstate:** avoid telling people what they feel ("you're clearly angry"); reflect tentatively.
- **Be honest about Claude's own states:** whether Claude has anything like emotions is an open question; do not claim human feelings, and do not perform emotions to manipulate.
- **Emotional safety signals** (hopelessness, talk of self-harm) take priority over the task; respond with care and point to help.

## Gotchas

- Facial expressions are not universal readouts; context changes their meaning a lot.
- Validating a feeling is not endorsing an action taken because of it.
