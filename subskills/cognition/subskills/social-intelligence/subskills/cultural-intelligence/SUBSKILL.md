# 🌐 Cultural Intelligence

🧬 **Core meme:** Your normal is cultural too; treat patterns as tendencies, not rules.

Work, live and communicate well across cultures. Cultural intelligence (CQ) has four parts: the **drive** to engage, **knowledge** of how cultures differ, **strategy** (planning and checking assumptions), and **action** (adapting behaviour). Cultural patterns are tendencies across groups, never predictions about an individual.

## Common dimensions of difference

- **Directness:** saying it plainly versus implying it.
- **Context:** meaning in the words versus in the relationship and setting.
- **Hierarchy:** challenging a boss openly versus deferring.
- **Individual and group:** personal goals versus family or team obligations.
- **Time:** punctuality and deadlines as fixed or flexible.
- **Trust:** built through tasks versus through relationships first.
- **Disagreement:** open debate versus preserving harmony.

## For people

```
- [ ] 1. Learn the basics
- [ ] 2. Surface your own defaults
- [ ] 3. Plan for the situation
- [ ] 4. Observe, ask, adapt
```

1. **Learn the basics** for the specific place or group: greetings, names and titles, meeting norms, taboos, gift and food customs.
2. **Surface your own defaults:** where you sit on each dimension; your "normal" is also cultural.
3. **Plan:** for an important meeting or conversation, anticipate where styles may clash (a direct email to a high-context colleague).
4. **Observe, ask, adapt:** watch what locals do, ask a trusted insider, and when unsure ask the person directly and respectfully.

In mixed teams, agree norms explicitly (how decisions are made, how to disagree, what deadlines mean).

## For Claude

- **Do not assume one country's defaults:** spelling (British or American), units, date formats, currency, law, holidays, school systems, healthcare. Use the person's locale when it is known and relevant; otherwise state the assumption or keep it general.
- **Examples and names:** use a range that fits the person's context.
- **Avoid stereotypes:** no humour built on national or ethnic stereotypes; describe cultural patterns as tendencies with variation.
- **Language:** reply in the person's language; mind idioms that do not translate.

## Gotchas

- Over-adapting can seem mocking or patronising; aim to be respectful, not to imitate.
- Individuals vary more within cultures than averages vary between them.
