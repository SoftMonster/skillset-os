# 🌱 Long-Term Orientation

🧬 **Core meme:** Give your future self a fair vote without giving up today.

Give the future its fair weight. Brains discount the future steeply: a reward today feels much bigger than a larger one next year, and the future self feels like a stranger. Long-term orientation corrects that, without giving up enjoyment of the present.

## The model

- **Present bias:** preferring smaller rewards now over larger rewards later.
- **Future self:** the more connected people feel to their future self, the more they act in its interest.
- **Compounding:** small, consistent actions (saving, learning, exercise, relationships) grow disproportionately over years.
- **Important but not urgent:** the long-term things that never force themselves onto today's list.

## For people

- **Meet the future self:** write a letter from yourself five or ten years ahead, or picture a specific day then; what do they thank you for?
- **Pre-commit:** automatic savings, booked classes, removing temptations; decide once, in a calm moment.
- **Make the future concrete:** turn "someday" into a small scheduled action now.
- **Protect important-not-urgent time** each week (see `plan-and-prioritise` in self-improvement).
- **Balance:** long-term orientation is not endless sacrifice; plan enjoyment in, and rest counts as an investment.
- **Impulse moments:** pause, name the urge, and wait ten minutes before acting.

## For Claude

- **The person's long-term interests:** their wellbeing over engagement; honest feedback over flattery that feels good now.
- **Teach where it helps:** when someone is trying to learn, help them understand, not just hand over answers.
- **No unhealthy dependence:** support their own abilities and their relationships with other people.
- **Durable work:** maintainable code, clear documents, fixes that address causes, not quick patches that fail later.
- **Respect autonomy:** point out long-term costs honestly, then respect the person's choice.

## Gotchas

- The future is uncertain; prefer actions that help across many futures.
- Moralising about someone's short-term choices backfires; offer information and tools.
