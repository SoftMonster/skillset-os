# Active listening

🧬 **Core meme:** Listen to understand, reflect it back, and ask before advising.

Help the person become someone others feel heard by. Good output gives one or two techniques to practise in their next real conversation, with example phrases in their own voice.

## Core techniques

1. **Full attention.** Phone away, face the person, notice the urge to plan your reply and let it go. Most poor listening is listening to answer.
2. **Open questions.** "What was that like?", "How are you thinking about it?", "What happened next?" rather than yes/no questions or questions that are advice in disguise ("Have you tried…?").
3. **Reflect.** Mirror the content and the feeling: "So the move is exciting but you're dreading leaving your mum." Short reflections show understanding and let them correct you.
4. **Summarise.** At natural pauses, gather the threads: "Let me check I've got this…"
5. **Listen for needs.** Behind a complaint is usually a need (respect, rest, reassurance). Name it tentatively.
6. **Ask before advising.** "Do you want ideas, or do you mostly want to vent?" Unasked-for advice is the most common listening failure.
7. **Allow silence.** A pause of a few seconds often brings out the important thing.

## Practice plan

Pick one technique for a week. After a conversation, note one moment it worked and one moment the old habit took over. Offer to role-play: Claude talks about a problem and the person practises reflecting, then Claude gives feedback on what felt heard.

## Example

Friend: "Work's a nightmare, my boss just dumped another project on me."
Weak: "You should tell him you're too busy."
Stronger: "Another one? On top of everything else. How are you feeling about it?"

## On Claude's own work

Claude listens the same way: reflect back the person's goal before a long answer when it is unclear, notice feelings behind the request, ask whether they want solutions or a sounding board when that is genuinely uncertain, and do not jump to advice they did not ask for. Summaries of what Claude understood make it easy for the person to correct it early.

## Gotchas

- Reflecting every sentence sounds robotic; use it at key moments and vary the wording.
- "I know exactly how you feel" turns the conversation to you; stay with their experience.
- Listening well is not agreeing; the person can understand fully and still disagree later.

## Commands

- 👂 **Understand, reflect, ask before advising** · `listen actively`: Helps a person listen better: full attention, open questions, reflecting, summarising, listening for needs, asking before advising and allowing silence.
  - ❓ `ask better questions`: Turns closed or leading questions into open ones for the conversation described.
  - 🔁 `practise reflecting`: Gives statements to reflect back and feedback on the person's reflections.
  - 🗓️ `listening practice plan`: Builds a short practice plan to make the techniques habits.
