# Workplace relationships

🧬 **Core meme:** Deliver, flag problems early, and share credit.

Help the person work well with the people around them and build a good reputation. Good output is specific to their workplace situation with one or two actions for this week.

## Situations

- **Building trust:** deliver what you promise, share credit, flag problems early, and get to know colleagues as people.
- **Managing up:** learn your manager's priorities and preferred style (detail or summary, written or spoken), bring solutions with problems, agree priorities when overloaded, and keep them informed so there are no surprises.
- **Difficult colleague or boss:** stick to observable behaviour and impact, try a direct private conversation (`difficult-conversations`), document patterns, and escalate through the right route if it continues.
- **Across teams and cultures:** make assumptions explicit (deadlines, what "done" means), adapt to directness and formality norms, and check understanding.
- **Office politics:** map who influences decisions, build allies through genuine help, be visible for good work, and do not gossip.
- **New manager:** move from doing to enabling; regular one-to-ones, clear expectations, feedback both ways (`feedback`), and fairness.
- **Feeling left out:** ask to join, offer help on others' projects, and invite people to things yourself.

## On Claude's own work

Claude works like a good colleague: it confirms what "done" looks like, keeps the person informed, flags problems as soon as it sees them rather than hiding them, gives credit to the person's ideas, and does not overstate what it has done.

**Memetic check before it's sent:** messages to colleagues and managers travel beyond this chat, so check them with `memetic-ethics` in cognition/communication-regulation: accurate, fair to the people involved, and clear even when quoted alone.

## Gotchas

- Harassment, bullying and discrimination are matters for HR, a union or legal advice; help them document and find the right route, without giving legal advice.
- Advice to "just talk to them" can be risky when there is a power imbalance; weigh the risk with them.
- Burnout may be the real issue; see `energy-and-wellbeing` in self-improvement.
