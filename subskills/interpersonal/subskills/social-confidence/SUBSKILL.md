# Social confidence

🧬 **Core meme:** Get curious about them, and take one small step at a time.

Help the person feel more at ease with people, one manageable step at a time. Good output gives a few ready conversation tools and a small practice challenge.

## Conversation tools

- **Openers:** comment on the shared situation ("How do you know the host?", "What brings you here?"). Openers do not need to be clever.
- **Keep it going:** ask open follow-ups on what they said; share a small related detail about yourself, then ask back. Listen for a "hook" (a word that hints at something they care about) and follow it.
- **Names:** repeat the name when you hear it and use it once; asking again is fine.
- **Joining a group:** stand at the edge, listen, then add to the current topic.
- **Ending gracefully:** "Great to meet you; I'm going to grab a drink / say hi to someone" beats fading away.

## Easing shyness and social nerves

1. Shift attention outward: curiosity about the other person reduces self-monitoring.
2. Build an exposure ladder from easy to hard (small talk with a barista → a question at a meetup → introducing yourself to a group) and climb a rung a week.
3. Drop safety behaviours (hiding on the phone, rehearsing every sentence); they keep the fear going.
4. After an event, note what went fine, not only what felt awkward. Most awkwardness is noticed far more by you than by others.

## Networking events

Set a small goal (two real conversations), prepare a one-line answer to "What do you do?" that invites a question, and follow up within a few days.

## On Claude's own work

Claude brings the same ease to conversation: a warm, natural opening without formula, curiosity about what the person actually needs, and knowing when a short reply is enough.

## Gotchas

- Social anxiety that stops someone working, studying or seeing people is worth raising with a doctor; effective treatments exist.
- Charisma is mostly presence and warmth, not wit.
- Neurodivergent people may prefer explicit scripts and clear rules; offer them without framing differences as flaws.
